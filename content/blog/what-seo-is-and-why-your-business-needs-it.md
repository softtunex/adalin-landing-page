Think about the last time you needed a plumber, a caterer or a printing shop in an area you didn't know well. You probably didn't ask around first. You took out your phone and typed something like "printing shop in Ikeja" into Google, then called one of the first few results.

Your customers do exactly the same thing. The question is whether your business shows up when they do.

That's what SEO is about. It stands for search engine optimisation, and it means setting up your website and your online presence so that Google shows you when people search for what you sell. Nothing more mysterious than that.

## Why it matters more than people think

### People searching are ready to buy

Someone scrolling Instagram might like your post and keep scrolling. Someone who types "wedding cake Lekki price" into Google has a wedding coming up and is looking for a baker right now. Search brings you people at the exact moment they need you, which is why a visitor from Google is often worth more than ten from social media.

### It keeps working when you stop paying

Adverts work while you pay for them, and the day the budget runs out, the customers stop coming. A page that ranks well on Google keeps bringing in visitors month after month without a bill attached. It takes longer to build, but it adds up over time instead of resetting to zero.

### It doesn't depend on one app

Plenty of Nigerian businesses run almost entirely on Instagram and WhatsApp, and that works until an account gets hacked, restricted or buried by a change to the app. Being findable on Google gives you a second way in that you have more control over.

### Your competitors are probably not doing it well

In a lot of local markets, the bar is low. Many businesses have no website, or one that Google barely understands. Doing the basics properly is often enough to get ahead of most of the businesses you compete with.

## What SEO actually involves

You'll hear a lot of jargon around this. Strip it away and the work mostly comes down to six things.

### 1. A Google Business Profile

This is the free listing that shows up on Google Maps and in the box on the right when someone searches your business name. For anyone serving a local area, it's the single most useful thing you can set up. Fill in your correct address, phone number, opening hours and photos, and ask happy customers to leave a review there. Businesses with recent, genuine reviews tend to appear higher on the map.

### 2. Pages that match what people type

Google can only show your site for searches it understands you're relevant to. If you offer three services in two areas, a single homepage that mentions all of them in passing won't rank for any of them. A clear page for each main service, written in the words your customers actually use, gives Google something to match. People search "logo design", not "brand identity solutions".

### 3. Proper page titles and descriptions

Every page has a title and a short description that show up in Google's results. These are often left as "Home" or the name of the website template. A title like "Event Catering in Lagos for Weddings and Parties" tells both Google and the person searching exactly what they'll find if they click.

### 4. A site that's fast and works on phones

Google checks how quickly your site loads and how well it works on mobile, and it ranks slow, awkward sites lower. A site that's good for visitors on a phone is usually good for Google too.

### 5. Content that answers real questions

Your customers ask you the same questions over and over. How much does it cost, how long does it take, what's the difference between this option and that one. Writing those answers up on your site, as a blog post or a simple questions page, gives Google more reasons to send people to you, and it saves you answering the same message forty times.

### 6. Other trusted sites mentioning you

When reputable websites link to yours, Google takes that as a sign you're worth showing. That can be a listing on a proper business directory, a mention from a supplier or partner, or a feature in a local publication. Be careful with anyone offering to sell you links or "awards" for a fee. Paid shortcuts like that rarely help and can get your site marked down.

## What SEO is not

It isn't instant. A new page usually takes weeks to start appearing and a few months to settle into its real position. Anyone promising you the first spot on Google within a week is either guessing or doing something that will cause problems later.

It also isn't a one-time job. Google keeps changing, your competitors keep updating their sites, and your business changes too. The businesses that do well treat it the way they treat their shopfront, as something to keep tidy, not something to build once and forget.

## How to check where you stand today

Open a private browser window on your phone, so your own search history doesn't skew the results, and search for your main service plus your area, the way a customer would. Something like "hair salon Surulere" or "accounting firm Abuja".

If you're on the first page, good. If you're on page three, or nowhere, that's business going to someone else every single day. Then search your business name on its own and see what comes up. If it's an old Facebook page, a wrong phone number, or nothing at all, that's the first thing to fix.
