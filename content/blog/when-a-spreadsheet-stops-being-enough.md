Almost every business starts the same way: a spreadsheet for orders, another for stock, maybe a notebook for who's owed what. It works, until one day it doesn't, and usually nobody notices the exact moment it stopped.

Here's how to tell you're past that point.

## Two people are editing the same file

Once more than one person needs to update the same sheet, you're already managing a small conflict every day. Someone overwrites someone else's update, or two people add the same order twice because neither could see what the other just did.

## You're afraid to open it on your phone

If checking stock or an order means "I'll do it when I'm at my laptop," the tool is working against you, not for you. A business doesn't pause just because you're not at a desk.

## The real numbers live in someone's head, not the sheet

This is the quiet one. The sheet says one thing, but the person running the business knows the sheet is behind, because updating it takes time nobody has in the middle of a busy day. At that point the spreadsheet isn't tracking the business anymore, it's just a record of whatever got typed in when there was a spare minute.

## Mistakes cost actual money

A missed stock update leads to promising something you don't have. A missed payment update leads to chasing money that was already paid, or not chasing money that wasn't. Small errors, but they compound every week.

## What actually fixes this

Not always a big system. Sometimes it's genuinely still a spreadsheet, just used properly with clear rules about who updates what. But once a business has real staff, real inventory, and real money moving through it daily, a proper system, one place for orders, stock, and payments that everyone sees the same version of, usually pays for itself within a few months just from the mistakes it stops.
